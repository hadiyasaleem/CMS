### 1.6 Related System Analysis / Literature Review

Putting academic information online is not new — learning platforms and college portals already do it, and their value is clear. What most of them do not do is connect all three sides of a college at once. A given tool is usually built for classroom teaching or for office administration, for one device type or one role, rather than giving administrators, teachers, and students matching mobile and desktop apps over the same records. Table 1-1 sets the closest comparisons against what this system does differently.

**Table 1-1 — Related System Analysis with Proposed Project Solution**

| Related system | Observed weakness | How this system responds |
|---|---|---|
| Google Classroom | Handles course communication and learning material well, but is not built to run a college's departments, academic sessions, fees, attendance reporting, or administrative approvals. | Keeps that academic and administrative record-keeping at its centre — departments, sessions, rosters, fees, attendance reports, and a lock-then-approve workflow for mark changes — while still carrying notices, datesheets, and a calendar through to each role. |
| General college-management portals | Tend to focus on office use or browser access, and often lack matching role-based applications for teachers and students across both mobile and desktop. | Ships six coordinated applications — an admin, a teacher, and a student app, each on Android and desktop — so every role gets a fitting experience on the device in front of them, all reading the same data. |
| Paper registers and noticeboards | Need physical access, repeated copying, manual totalling, and separate re-checking whenever something changes. | Records each figure once, makes an approved change visible to the affected users, and keeps a clear, named responsibility for the actions that matter. |
